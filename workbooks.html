♟ Nursery 2

First Term

Meet the Pieces & the Board

1. Chess is a game played by how many players?

(a) One   (b) Two   (c) Three

Answer: (b) Two
Why? Chess is a two-player game. One player controls the white pieces and the other controls the black pieces.

2. What is the name of the tallest piece that looks like a crown?

(a) Pawn   (b) King   (c) Rook

Answer: (b) King
Remember: The King is the most important piece. If he is checkmated, you lose the game.

3. What is the name of the piece that looks like a horse?

Answer: Knight
The Knight is the only piece that can jump over other pieces. It moves in an “L” shape.

4. What is the name of the piece that looks like a castle tower?

Answer: Rook

5. The small piece that stands in front is called a:

Answer: Pawn
Each side has 8 pawns. They are the soldiers of the army.

6. How many white squares are on a chessboard?

Answer: 32
A chessboard has 64 squares – 32 light and 32 dark.

7. When we set up the board, the White Queen sits on a:

Answer: White square
Golden rule: “Queen on her own colour.” White Queen on light square, Black Queen on dark square.

8. Name the six chess pieces.

Answer: King, Queen, Rook, Bishop, Knight, Pawn
Second Term

Files, Ranks & Diagonals

1. Vertical lines on the chessboard are called:

Answer: Files (a to h)

2. Horizontal lines are called:

Answer: Ranks (1 to 8)

3. Lines that go slanting are called:

Answer: Diagonals

4. The square a1 is in which corner for White?

Answer: Bottom-left
Always place the board so that the light square is on your right-hand corner (“light on right”).

5. Which piece is the most powerful?

Answer: Queen
The Queen can move any number of squares vertically, horizontally or diagonally.
Third Term

Piece Values

Point values to remember:

Pawn = 1  •  Knight = 3  •  Bishop = 3
Rook = 5  •  Queen = 9  •  King = Priceless
These numbers help you decide whether a trade is good or bad. Never give away a Queen for a Pawn!

Can the King be captured and taken off the board?

Answer: No
The King is never captured. When he has no escape from check, the game ends by checkmate.

♞ Grade 1

First Term

How Pieces Move

A Pawn moves how many steps forward on its first move?

Answer: 1 or 2

A Pawn captures by moving:

Answer: One step diagonally forward

A Rook moves:

Answer: Straight along files and ranks (+)

Which piece can jump over other pieces?

Answer: Knight
The Knight’s L-shape is 2 squares in one direction and then 1 square sideways.
Second Term

Open, Closed & Semi-Open Files + Check

An Open File has:

Answer: No pawns of either colour
Rooks and Queens love open files – they can travel the whole way.

When the King is attacked, we say the King is in:

Answer: Check

Name one way to get out of check.

Answer: Move the King / Capture the attacker / Block (Interpose)
Third Term

The MIC Rule & Checkmate

What does MIC stand for?

Answer: Move the King • Interpose (block) • Capture the attacker
These are the only three legal ways to get out of check. If none works → Checkmate!

Checkmate happens when the King is in check and:

Answer: Cannot be saved by Move, Interpose or Capture

♝ Grade 2 – Checkmate Patterns & Draws

Key Ideas

Ladder Mate

Two Rooks (or Rook + Queen) work together like a ladder, pushing the enemy King to the edge of the board until he has no squares left.

Stalemate happens when:

The side to move is not in check but has no legal moves.
Stalemate is a draw. Be careful not to take away all the King’s squares when he is not in check!

Five ways a game can end in a draw

1. Stalemate
2. Insufficient material (e.g. King vs King)
3. Threefold repetition
4. 50-move rule
5. Agreement / Perpetual check

What is Zugzwang?

You are forced to make a move and every possible move makes your position worse.

♜ Grade 3–4 – Strategy & Endgames

Important Concepts

Opposition

When two Kings face each other with exactly one square between them. The player who does not have to move has the opposition and can often force the other King backwards.

Rule of the Square

Helps you know if a King can catch a passed pawn. Imagine a square from the pawn to the promotion square; if the enemy King can step inside that square, he can catch the pawn.

Openings vs Defenses

Opening = system of moves played by White (who moves first).
Defense = setup played by Black to counter White’s opening.

Famous opening starts

Italian Game: 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4
Sicilian Defense: 1.e4 c5

♛ Grade 5 – Advanced Ideas

Centre, Titles, En Passant & Tactics

Primary central squares

e4, d4, e5, d5
Controlling the centre gives your pieces more mobility and makes it harder for the opponent to develop.

FIDE titles (lowest to highest)

CM → FM → IM → GM (Grandmaster)

En Passant – three strict conditions

1. Your pawn must be on the 5th rank (White) or 4th rank (Black).
2. The opponent just moved a pawn two squares from its starting rank, landing beside your pawn.
3. You must capture immediately on the next move or the right is lost.

Weak square / Hole

A square that can no longer be defended by any pawns. Knights love to sit on holes (outposts) because they cannot be chased away by pawns.

Interference vs Clearance

Interference: Place or sacrifice a piece to block a defensive line.
Clearance: Move your own piece out of the way (often with tempo) to free a line or square for another piece.

Why analyse your games after they finish?

A post-mortem helps you find mistakes, understand better moves, and improve much faster than just playing more games.
